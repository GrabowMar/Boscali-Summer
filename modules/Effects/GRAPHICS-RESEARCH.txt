Graphical improvement candidates — 2026-11-15
============================================
Target: Nuclear Option 0.34.3, Unity 2022.3, URP 14.0.12; a BepInEx mod
of an already-built game. Package Manager installation alone cannot add
renderer features or recover shader variants stripped from that game.

Implemented in this change
--------------------------
meenphie's AgX LUTs: BASE, PUNCHY, HUE; independent live toggle, default on.
Compose the native linear HDR grading LUT before output, after native grading.
The actual game world and cockpit cameras do not enable post-processing;
postProcessingRenderer is their final post-processing overlay. Follow that
owner so the combined frame is graded once. Exposure and G-LOC stay native.
Night vision, sensor cameras, LDR targets and HDR display output bypass AgX.
https://github.com/meenphie/AgX-Tonemapping-Unity
Exact source revision and missing licence: Assets/PROVENANCE.txt.

Optional BF4-inspired cinematic preset, default off: cool shadows, restrained
warm highlights, deeper shadow contrast, exposure-relative bloom, horizontal
HDR light streaks, subtle vignette and custom grain. Strength and LIGHT GLOW
are adjustable in SET > WORLD > COLOUR GRADE. No motion blur or depth of field.
The streak pass runs on the world base camera before the final overlay's post.
The native URP volume runs below night vision, and it never owns exposure or
the saturation parameter used by G-LOC. Built-in RCAS sharpening and display
.cube loading were already present and are reused.

Further controls
----------------
1. Built-in URP dithering: SKY DITHER, default on. `CameraPostQuality` sets the
   base camera's dithering property; the final overlay inherits it. Setting
   the final overlay component alone would do nothing. The service validates
   native uber/final shaders and every blue-noise texture before applying,
   restoring the native field when the override is off or resources vanish.
   Runs after final sharpening and cannot repair banding already quantized
   into an earlier intermediate texture.
   https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@14.0/api/UnityEngine.Rendering.Universal.UniversalAdditionalCameraData.html

2. Built-in SMAA: EDGE SMOOTHING > SMAA, opt-in; GAME restores native AA.
   Sets base camera AA and High quality, inherited by the final overlay.
   Validates its supported three-pass shader and both area/search textures.
   Adds three full-resolution passes before bloom and tonemapping; compatible
   with the existing later FSR/RCAS pass. Native values restore on disable,
   scene/camera swap and release. TAA is a poorer drop-in fit:
   URP 14 TAA does not support camera stacking, MSAA or dynamic resolution.
   https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@14.0/manual/anti-aliasing.html

   Validation on 2026-11-15: Release build passed with zero warnings/errors;
   25 focused Unity assertions passed for resources, native camera-stack
   inheritance, sensor isolation and restoration. A rendered 1080p flight
   passed 12 live checks across native, dither, SMAA, sharpen and SMAA+sharpen
   states, with no reported graphics errors. Saved captures and frame windows:
   C:/Users/marci/.nomodkit/runs/20261115-220311-77ca-sim-post-quality
   This sequential smoke check is not a controlled performance benchmark.

Other candidates (researched; not installed)
------------------------------------------
3. Built-in SSAO. Medium effort: runtime renderer feature/resources, downsample
   and depth reconstruction first. Could add contact depth around aircraft,
   cockpit recesses and compounds, but verify custom material normals and
   camera-stack interaction. Downsampling reduces AO pixel count fourfold.
   Shipped URP DLL contains the internal feature/settings; reflection can
   create it. A pilot could use InterleavedGradient, Depth, AfterOpaque,
   Low samples and Medium blur. AfterOpaque avoids surface shader variants,
   but multiplies all opaque light, including direct/emissive light. Validate
   the retained shader variants, cockpit depth clearing, no duplicate native
   AO, silhouettes and cost before enabling. A mod-owned wrapper on the live
   renderer feature list avoids rebuilding/disposal of the whole renderer.
   If vendoring a stripped shader, pin URP14.0.12 and preserve its Unity
   Companion License; those shader sources are not MIT.
   https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@14.0/manual/post-processing-ssao.html

4. CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light. MIT, Unity 2022.3+, DX11, forward and
   deferred. Most promising external renderer feature for visible light beams.
   Medium/high effort: bundle its shaders and connect it to existing Weather
   fog/clouds rather than introduce a second atmosphere. Transparent-object
   blending is limited; multiple local lights add GPU cost.
   https://github.com/CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light
   https://github.com/CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light/blob/main/LICENSE.md

5. jiaozi158/UnitySSReflectionURP. MIT, Unity 2022.2+/URP 14+. Conditional
   prototype, not drop-in: its setup requires Deferred variants retained at
   original build time, even for Forward. Those may be stripped from the game.
   Ignores transparent objects; not a guaranteed improvement to the ocean.
   https://github.com/jiaozi158/UnitySSReflectionURP
   https://github.com/jiaozi158/UnitySSReflectionURP/blob/main/Documentation/Documentation.md
   https://github.com/jiaozi158/UnitySSReflectionURP/blob/main/LICENSE

6. jiaozi158/UnitySSGIURP. MIT, Unity 2022.3.35f1+/URP14. High effort,
   experimental: screen-space bounce light needs temporal/motion-vector
   checks, with possible ghosting. Less benefit over sky and distant terrain.
   https://github.com/jiaozi158/UnitySSGIURP
   https://github.com/jiaozi158/UnitySSGIURP/blob/main/LICENSE.md

Existing Weather already supplies volumetric clouds, cloud shadows, fog,
ocean waves, sky phenomena and high-altitude atmosphere. Replacing those
is a substantial feature change. Third-party candidates above were researched
from their own documentation; no runtime compatibility or performance claim
is made for packages not integrated here.
